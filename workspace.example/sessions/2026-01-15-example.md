# Session: 2026-01-15 — Vector dot products

## Outcome
Given a query vector and two candidate key vectors, compute both dot products and
justify which candidate has the larger compatibility score.

## Diagnostic evidence
The learner initially claimed that `a · b` was "the angle between the vectors."
Asked what units and range an angle should have, the learner noticed that
`(2, 0) · (3, 0) = 6` cannot itself be an angle.

## Learning route
1. Vector components -> dot-product calculation: corresponding components supply
   the products that are summed.
2. Dot-product calculation -> geometric interpretation: the sign and magnitude
   can be checked against the vectors' alignment.
3. Geometric interpretation -> compatibility comparison: the two scores can be
   compared without yet introducing a full attention mechanism.

## Concepts established
Asked which part of
`a · b = ||a|| ||b|| cos(theta)` carries angular information, the learner
revised the claim: the dot product combines alignment with both magnitudes.

## Application evidence
For an application, the learner used query `(1, 2)` with candidates `(2, 1)`
and `(-1, 2)`, calculated scores `4` and `3`, and selected the first candidate.
The learner justified the choice by comparing the computed scores rather than
by self-report.

## Remaining gaps
The learner has not yet explained, without prompting, how scaling a key changes
an unnormalized compatibility score. No evidence about softmax or scaled
dot-product attention was collected.

## Next step
Predict how doubling one candidate changes its score, justify the prediction,
then verify it by calculation.

## Sources
No external sources were used; the session relied on direct calculations shown
in the record.
